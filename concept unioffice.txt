The core concept we had been building was not just another chatbot or AI assistant. It was an AI-native company operating system: a coordinated organization of specialized agents that can perform actual company functions.

The product model we had established was roughly:

And the important part was the coordination layer:

Company Brain — shared organizational knowledge/context.
Agents — specialized workers rather than generic chatbots.
Memory — persistent organizational knowledge.
Communication — agents coordinating across departments.
Workflows — agents executing multi-step business processes.
Approval gates — humans remain in control of consequential actions.
Organization map — visual representation of the AI workforce.
Agent performance — tracking what agents are doing and how well.
Cross-team coordination — agents can hand work between departments.

We also explicitly framed the product around:

“Hire agents, not seats.”

That's why the landing page now has the 48-agent Team plan, 137-agent Company plan, and custom Enterprise model.

And this is where I would be careful: we shouldn't jump straight from the landing page into building 137 agents. That's the wrong engineering order.

The first real UNI-Office product should prove the core operating-system loop with a small number of agents:

task → understand context → plan → delegate → execute → communicate → request approval when necessary → record outcome → learn/store memory.

Once that architecture actually works, adding dozens of specialized agents becomes an expansion problem rather than a mess of 137 independent AI prompts.

So yes, I remember what we're building. Ultron → UNI-Office was a rebrand, not a change in the fundamental product vision.
